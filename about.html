About me

I'm Sean Heakes, an astronomer and researcher with a passion for radio astronomy, atmospheric modeling, and hands-on instrument building.

I studied astronomy at the University of British Columbia (B.Sc., 2021–2026). My academic journey has been shaped by a love of exploration and hands-on problem-solving: I am as happy at a lathe or a soldering iron as I am writing Python.

In 2024 I spent two terms at the Dominion Radio Astrophysical Observatory in Kaleden, BC. As a junior mechanical design engineer I led a drone photogrammetry project on the 26-metre John A. Galt radio telescope, and as an astronomy co-op student I wrote a pipeline that convolves the telescope's simulated polarized beam with the Effelsberg-Bonn HI Survey. Before that I helped commission the Thunderbird South telescope under UBC Professor Aaron Boley.

Since 2023 I have been Equipment Coordinator for the UBC Astronomy Club, where I look after the club's telescopes, test their optics, and help write grants for new equipment. I also served as Astronomy Representative on the UBC Science Undergraduate Society from 2023 to 2024.

Skills and training

  • Python for data analysis, coordinate transforms and simulation
  • Radio astronomy: 21 cm hydrogen observations, interferometry (NRAO Synthesis Imaging Workshop, 2024)
  • Optical testing: double-pass autocollimation and Ronchi gratings
  • Machining: UBC Physics and Astronomy Student Machine Shop Course (35 hours, 2022)
  • Electronics: UBC PCB Design and Prototyping course (30 hours, 2023)
